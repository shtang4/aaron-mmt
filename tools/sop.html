MMT One-Page SOP

Only what the course itself states, minus what it contradicts elsewhere. Bybit USDT perpetuals, 1-minute entry chart, direction from the higher timeframes. Every step is a rule you can check; if you cannot tick it, you skip.

1 Scan the market Diagnose

  1. Heatmap. Mostly green → long bias. Mostly red → short bias. Mixed → no trades today.
  2. Hot sectors → the leading coin in each. Then Top Movers (24h, and the 15m tabs) for candidates. A gainer is a candidate, never a trade.
  3. Screener, scored out of 4: EMA 20 above/below on 1D + 4H + 1H · OI rising with price · funding on your side (≤ −0.01% long, ≥ +0.05% short; positive accepted on longs in a bull) · book lighter on your side. 4/4 full size · 3/4 at 50–75% with a tighter stop · below 3, no trade. Mixed timeframes → reduce size.
  4. Sentiment gauge is a clue, not a signal. Greed ≠ buy.

2 Set direction Diagnose

  1. EMA 155 rule, on the Heikin-Ashi chart: three consecutive candles with the body above and no lower wick → long only; body below and no upper wick → short only. Nothing else sets direction. Order prices still from real candles.
  2. Heikin-Ashi run with no trend-side wicks confirms. Dojis or small-body clusters → wait.
  3. Donchian ribbon solid in the same colour confirms. Mixed → skip.
  4. Seahorse (vertical move done, flat beak, EMAs converging) → stay out.
  5. Hockey stick (slow decline, sharp V, vertical blade): enter only on the golden cross with the EMA 155 three-candle rule. Never buy the blade.

3 Sniper entry Diagnose · SPE

  1. TradingView: nearest demand zone below (long) or supply zone above (short), and any FVG. Take the near edge: top of demand, bottom of supply.
  2. Bybit: EMA 9 is the first target; if the zone edge sits deeper, the EMA closest to the edge. That is the price.
  3. Rest a limit order there. Re-place it if the EMA moves. Never market-buy the candle that is running. Wait at the lounge.
  4. Correlated alts making the same move at the same minute are one trade. Size the total, not each ticket.

4 Size and stop Dose

  1. Isolated margin. Leverage 5× to 10×.
  2. Margin per trade: $20 to learn; then 1% of wallet; never above 10%. Loss at the stop never above 1–2% of equity, whatever the score.
  3. Stop as ROI on margin, by tier: beginner 5–20%, intermediate 20–50%, advanced 50%+. Convert to price; it must sit beyond the structural level (far edge of the zone, or the heel low). If it does not, lower the leverage until it does. Never move the level, never widen the tier.
  4. Write the money at risk in USDT before entry. If the number makes you flinch, the dose is wrong.

5 Exit plan, both ways Discharge

  1. TP first. Wick Tracker is a Bybit TP trigger at a level a wick will reach: prior wick high, supply-zone bottom, liquidation cluster, or 50% ROI raised to 55–60% for fees. Trigger on Last; set it slightly inside the expected wick so it fills.
  2. SL from step 4, trigger on Mark, resting before the entry fills. Both in before you look away.
  3. Climax exit: volume bar tallest since entry and ≥ 2× VOLMA 10 (working rule) + MACD histogram at its tallest bar, confirmed when the next bar prints shorter → close 75%. Move SL on the runner to entry.
  4. Runner: Hunting SL locks a rising ROI, entry ± entry × ROI ÷ leverage. EMA 9 crossing back through EMA 25 is the backstop: exit, no questions.
  5. Stop hit = the plan worked. Take it. Nothing replaces it.

6 After the close Follow up

  1. Sweep realised profit from Derivatives to Funding.
  2. Close and go. Next coin. Never re-enter the same beak. Nothing is held past 24 hours.
  3. Stop at the daily target or ten trades, whichever first. Rest.
  4. Log it in the journal: which D, which rule, what you would change. Thirty trades before you believe any number.

7 When it goes wrong Discharge

  1. The stop is the answer. It was written before entry; honour it.
  2. If you skipped the stop and are underwater: close it. The loss is the loss.
  3. If you want the opposite direction: close the loser first, open the new trade as its own sized position with its own stop. Never both legs at once.
  4. The one hedging case: a position opened in cross mode by mistake. Hedge to freeze exposure, then close it or switch it to isolated, then close the hedge.
  5. News, FOMC, black-swan candles: no method is 100%. Smaller dose, or flat.

Never, because the course's own risk module says so

  • Cross margin. Liquidation takes the whole wallet.
  • A position without a resting stop. "Set it before the entry order fills."
  • Hedging instead of taking the stop. The only hedge is on an accidental cross-mode position, and it ends with closing it.
  • Holding a loser to breakeven. Upside zero, downside the account.
  • Trading a mixed heatmap or a seahorse.
  • Entering on a chat call without the chart check. Late trade = losing trade.
  • Holding for days. This is not swing trading.
  • New listings beyond a few days; no EMA 155 to lean on.
  • Adding size after a loss. Too much kills.
  • Blaming mindset before checking which D you skipped.
